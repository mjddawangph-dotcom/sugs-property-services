What landscaping means in the High Desert

Most yards out here do not start as a blank canvas with good soil. They start as bare dirt, gravel that has gone weedy, or a lawn that gave up two summers ago. So the work tends to fall into three groups: resetting a yard that has gotten away, installing the pieces that hold it together — sod, rock, planters, irrigation — and keeping it maintained afterward.

Sug's Property Services is an owner-operated property services business, not a design-build contractor. We take on the resets, the installs, and the upkeep: the practical end of landscaping. If your project needs architectural drawings, a 3D render, or major hardscape construction, we will tell you honestly and point you toward someone who does that work.

How a landscaping job runs

  1. We walk the property. Soil, slope, drainage, sun exposure, existing irrigation, and how a truck and trailer get to the work area. This is where most surprises are found.
  2. You get a written scope and price. What is included, what is not, and what the number is. No work starts before you agree to it.
  3. Prep comes first. Clearing, weed removal, grading, and shaping. A yard that is not prepped properly will not hold sod, rock, or plantings for long.
  4. Install. Sod, rock and gravel, planting, and any irrigation work the layout needs.
  5. Handoff. We tell you how to water it, when to mow it for the first time, and what to expect in the first few weeks.

What moves the price

Two properties the same size can quote very differently. The things that change the number most:

  • Lot size and layout — total area, and how much of it is narrow side yard.
  • Starting condition — knee-high weeds, packed gravel, and years of debris all cost labor.
  • Access — gate width, slope, stairs, and how far material has to be carried.
  • Irrigation condition — whether the existing system works, or needs repair before anything is planted.
  • Materials — sod, rock, and plants price out very differently per square foot.
  • Haul-away — green waste and old material have to go somewhere.

Landscaping versus lawn care

They overlap, and homeowners often use the words interchangeably. Lawn care is the recurring work — mowing, edging, and trimming on a schedule. Landscaping is the change: a yard cleaned up, sod put down, rock and beds rebuilt, irrigation fixed so the new work survives.

Most of our landscaping customers start with a cleanup and then move onto a maintenance schedule, because a reset yard only stays reset if somebody keeps up with it.
