Sprinkler and irrigation repair in Victorville

When part of the yard is dying while the rest is fine, the irrigation system is usually the reason — and usually a fixable one.

Common problems we are called for

  • Broken or tilted heads from mowers, foot traffic, or roots
  • Leaks at fittings, risers, and along the line
  • Stuck or failed valves that leave a zone running or dead
  • Controller and timer faults, including schedules that do not match the season
  • Dry spots caused by poor coverage rather than a lack of water
  • Overspray onto walls, drives, and sidewalks

A dry patch is not always a broken part. Sometimes the head is fine and the coverage is simply wrong, or the run time was set for summer and never adjusted. Finding that out first is cheaper than replacing parts until something works.

How we approach a repair

  1. Find the actual fault. Zone by zone, so we are not guessing.
  2. Fix or replace. Heads, valves, fittings, and controllers as needed.
  3. Retest coverage. A repair is not done until the zone waters evenly.
  4. Adjust the schedule. Water where it is needed, and stop paying to water the driveway.

Water is the expensive part

In the High Desert, irrigation is usually the largest part of a water bill. A leaking valve or a head spraying the block wall can waste water all season without ever looking like a problem. Fixing the hardware and correcting the schedule is often the cheapest efficiency upgrade a property has available.

If you are planning new sod or a yard reset, check the irrigation first. New plantings fail when the system underneath them cannot cover the area.

Questions

Irrigation questions

One part of my yard is dying. Can you find out why?

That is the usual call. We check the zone by zone coverage, look for a head that is clogged, tilted, or broken, and check whether the run time matches what the plants actually need. Dry spots usually have a cause, not a mystery.

Is $85 a full repair price?

$85 is the starting point for diagnosis and minor repair labor. Parts and larger repairs are quoted separately, and we talk to you before replacing anything significant.

Do you install new sprinkler systems?

Our focus is repairing and adjusting existing systems. If you need a complete new install, tell us what the property needs and we will be honest about whether it is a job we take on.

Can you fix a system that was installed by someone else?

Yes. We work on existing systems regardless of who put them in. We just need to know where the valves and controller are, and whether there is a shutoff you can reach.

How do I know if I am overwatering?

Runoff, standing water, mushrooms, and a lawn that stays soggy long after a cycle are the usual signs. In the High Desert, overspray onto walls and pavement is the other common one, and it is wasted water you are paying for.
